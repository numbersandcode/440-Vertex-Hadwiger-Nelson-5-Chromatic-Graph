[# G440: a 5-chromatic unit-distance graph with 440 vertices

G440 is a set of 440 points in the plane, with an edge between every two points at distance exactly 1 (2,099 edges), that cannot be coloured with four colours so that the ends of every edge differ. It was found on 23 November 2026 and certified the same day with standard tools (kissat, drat-trim, cake_lpr).

It stands at the end of a line of graphs in J. K. Haugland's heptagon lattice: Haugland (2,131 vertices), Wong (1,501), Sergi Galán (852) and Chandan Shukla (557), whose graph was our starting point. Aubrey de Grey's first 5-chromatic unit-distance graph (2018) and Marijn Heule's computer-checked reductions opened the way for all of them.

Heule and Przybocki's G449 (449 vertices, posted on 21 November 2026, same lattice) was grown outward from a small core and then whittled down. 

This 440 was reached the other way: by walking down from Shukla's graph in small exchanges of points, and by overlaying a graph with its seven rotated copies to work with fully symmetric graphs along the way. Its closest fully symmetric relative, G442 (442 vertices, 2,100 edges, sevenfold symmetric, 430 points in common with G440), is included.
